# Daily LinkedIn ideas

## Thought leadership ideas

1. **Most AI roadmaps fail before the pilot starts.**
   - **Re-hook:** The blocker is rarely the model.
   - **Angle:** Executive alignment usually lags behind tooling speed, so the organization never agrees on what success looks like, who owns decisions, or how adoption will work.
   - **Why it works:** It opens a curiosity gap, then moves straight to leadership alignment and operating model friction.

2. **Your AI ROI problem may actually be a governance problem.**
   - **Re-hook:** If no one owns the decision rules, no one owns the outcome.
   - **Angle:** Show how weak governance turns AI into a pile of pilots, conflicting metrics, and delayed decisions — and why clear ownership is what makes ROI measurable.
   - **Why it works:** It reframes a familiar business complaint into a sharper executive tension.

3. **The fastest way to kill AI adoption is to make it a tools project.**
   - **Re-hook:** Executives buy outcomes, not software.
   - **Angle:** AI adoption improves when leaders position it as an operating change: decision rights, workflows, enablement, and escalation paths — not just a software rollout.
   - **Why it works:** It challenges common implementation thinking without sounding abstract.

4. **The companies winning with AI are not moving faster. They are removing friction.**
   - **Re-hook:** Fewer approvals. Clearer handoffs. Better defaults.
   - **Angle:** Focus on adoption friction: too many approvals, unclear ownership, and disconnected workflows create drag that looks like “resistance” but is really system design.
   - **Why it works:** It makes the hidden cost of slow adoption tangible for leadership teams.

5. **If your AI pilot needs heroics, your operating model is broken.**
   - **Re-hook:** The process, not the model, is the bottleneck.
   - **Angle:** Use a short story about teams that rely on a few champions to keep AI moving, then explain why sustainable adoption depends on roles, routines, and governance that survive beyond the pilot.
   - **Why it works:** It’s memorable, executive-friendly, and directly tied to operating model design.

## Outreach hooks

1. Many leadership teams are increasing AI spend without a shared operating model. That gap is where execution usually slips.
2. Executive buyers are asking for AI ROI, but the real bottleneck is often decision rights. Curious if that shows up in your organization too.
3. The strongest AI programs I see are not the ones with the most tools. They are the ones with the clearest governance.

## Recommended post today

Post idea 2 today: it is the most directly tied to executive ROI, creates a strong curiosity gap, and gives you a clean path into governance without sounding technical.
