# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-08-23

## 1) AI pilots don't fail because the model is bad
**Hook:** AI pilots don't usually fail because the model is bad.
They fail because nobody owns the operating model.

**Re-hook:** That's the part most exec teams skip.

**Idea:** Write about how enterprise AI success depends less on picking the "best" model and more on assigning decision rights, escalation paths, data ownership, and success metrics. The post should contrast a flashy pilot with a repeatable operating cadence.

**Why it works:** It challenges the common assumption that model choice is the hard part, then shifts attention to execution friction senior leaders actually control.

---

## 2) Governance is what makes AI scalable, not slower
**Hook:** Most leaders treat AI governance like a brake.
That's the wrong metaphor.

**Re-hook:** Good governance is what makes AI repeatable.

**Idea:** Frame governance as the system that lets teams move faster with fewer exceptions. Cover lightweight policy, approved use cases, human review thresholds, auditability, and the difference between "no" governance and "safe yes" governance.

**Why it works:** It reframes a feared topic into an enabler of scale, which is more interesting to executives than another cautionary warning.

---

## 3) ROI is being measured too early
**Hook:** A lot of AI ROI stories are broken.
They're measuring the wrong thing too early.

**Re-hook:** Adoption comes before financial payoff.

**Idea:** Explain the sequence: first task-level savings, then workflow adoption, then operating leverage, then financial ROI. The point is to show why executives should not judge an AI program only on near-term cost takeout.

**Why it works:** It gives leaders a more realistic scorecard and helps them avoid killing promising programs before behavior changes have time to compound.

---

## 4) Adoption friction is the real budget line item
**Hook:** The biggest hidden cost in AI isn't inference.
It's adoption friction.

**Re-hook:** If people won't use it, nothing else matters.

**Idea:** Talk about the unglamorous blockers: workflow switching, trust, training, change fatigue, and unclear ownership. Position adoption friction as the thing that quietly turns good AI investments into shelfware.

**Why it works:** It names the silent failure mode leaders recognize immediately, while staying grounded in operational reality rather than hype.

---

## 5) The best AI strategy is not "AI everywhere"
**Hook:** "AI everywhere" is not a strategy.
It's how teams create chaos.

**Re-hook:** Start with one high-friction workflow.

**Idea:** Recommend choosing one workflow with clear pain, measurable ROI, and a realistic path to adoption. Show why a narrow, well-governed wedge beats a broad rollout that confuses teams and dilutes attention.

**Why it works:** It is contrarian, practical, and executive-friendly. It turns ambition into prioritization.
