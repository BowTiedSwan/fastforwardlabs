# Daily LinkedIn ideas

## Thought leadership ideas

1. **Multi-agent AI systems cost fifteen times more to run than single-model approaches — and most enterprise budgets were not built for that.** Anthropic's own internal research, surfaced this week, quantifies a pattern that practitioners have been observing informally: orchestrating multiple AI agents concurrently generates token consumption that is an order of magnitude higher than equivalent monolithic model usage. This is not a theoretical projection — it is a measured cost coefficient from the team building the systems. For executives who approved AI budgets based on per-seat licensing or single-model API costs, multi-agent architecture represents a category of spend that was not in the model. The organizations that will manage this well are the ones whose finance and technology leadership have agreed on a cost-per-outcome framework before scaling agent pipelines, not after the compute bill arrives.
   - Source: https://stax.sh/news/posts/anthropic-defaults-spend-more

2. **The frontier model providers are now competing on whether very large models can run on your hardware — and that changes the enterprise negotiating position materially.** StepFun's release of a 196-billion-parameter multimodal model that runs on a single 128 GB RAM machine is not primarily a technology story. It is a market signal. When models of this scale can be self-hosted without cloud infrastructure, the pricing leverage that API-first AI vendors have held over enterprise buyers begins to erode. Organizations currently locked into multi-year cloud AI API agreements should be tracking the local deployment frontier closely — not because they will immediately migrate, but because the credible alternative changes what they can negotiate. The executives who understand this shift will enter their next vendor renewal cycle with better information than the ones who treated local model deployment as a developer hobby.
   - Source: https://static.stepfun.com/blog/step-3.7-flash/

3. **Retrieval-augmented generation is no longer an advanced pattern — it is a production standard, and organizations that are still evaluating it are already behind.** The publication of a dedicated O'Reilly production guide for RAG systems is a reliable signal of maturity: it means enough organizations have moved through the failure modes to generate codifiable lessons. RAG — connecting AI models to curated, up-to-date organizational knowledge rather than relying on training data alone — is the architecture that closes the gap between a capable general model and a useful enterprise tool. The organizations that have not yet implemented RAG against their proprietary knowledge bases are not in evaluation mode; they are ceding ground to competitors who are already compounding value on top of the pattern. The question for leadership is not whether RAG belongs in the AI roadmap. It is whether the knowledge curation and governance work required to make RAG trustworthy has been resourced.
   - Source: https://www.reddit.com/r/Rag/comments/1tqby53/book_announcement_handson_rag_for_production/

4. **Agent frameworks are maturing faster than enterprise governance policies — and the accountability gap is widening.** The release of Hermes Agent v0.15.0 with improved memory handling and tighter vector store integration represents the kind of incremental but compounding capability unlock that characterizes a maturing open-source ecosystem. The practical implication is that building complex, multi-step autonomous workflows is becoming faster and cheaper for any team with engineering capacity — including teams inside your organization acting without central oversight. The governance challenge for 2026 is not just managing the AI systems that IT has approved and deployed. It is managing the AI systems that business units are assembling from open-source components because the approved systems are too slow to provision or too constrained to be useful. Shadow AI is not a future risk. It is already the default behavior in most large organizations, and the policy gap is growing every quarter.
   - Source: https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/releases/tag/v2026.5.28

5. **Workflow clarity before tool selection is the discipline that separates AI projects that compound from AI projects that stall.** The AI practitioner community is converging on a consistent post-mortem finding: the most common reason AI automation projects fail is not model capability — it is the absence of a clearly defined workflow before tool selection begins. Organizations that start with "which AI tool should we use" are making a sequencing error. The correct starting point is a precise description of the workflow being automated, including who owns each step, what success looks like, and where human judgment must remain in the loop. Teams that skip this step and proceed directly to tool selection are solving for adoption rather than outcomes. The AI projects that deliver durable business value in 2026 will be the ones where a leader — not a vendor — owned the workflow definition first.
   - Source: https://stax.sh/news/posts/anthropic-defaults-spend-more
